\begin{abstract}
This note presents the formalization of the paper of Heeger, Hermelin, Itzhaki, Molter and
Shabtay~\cite{HHIMS}: $n$ clients each submit one just-in-time job on every one of $m$
days, and a schedule is \emph{fair} if every client is served on at least $k$ of the days.
The paper gives a complete dichotomy of the problem's complexity in the fairness parameter
$k$, settles the two natural restrictions to day-independent processing times and
day-independent due dates, and determines the complexity with respect to the treewidth of
the daily conflict structure and the number of clients. Thirteen theorems, two lemmas and a
corollary are proved in Lean, together with a repair of the main treewidth construction
— the published gadget relies on an identification of jobs that in fact inflates the
treewidth it claims, repaired here by giving every blocked client its own private slot
— and a reduction of \textsc{Multicoloured Independent Set} on regular graphs to
\SAT, since the paper's own appeal to this hardness is unsubstantiated for the regular
instances its gadget needs. This note gives the statements in the notation
of~\cite{HHIMS} and links each to its Lean counterpart; it is not a substitute for the
paper, which motivates and develops the arguments this note only states.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% lax begin Lax117284.Scheduling
\begin{definition}[$1\mid\mathrm{rep}\mid\min_j\sum_iZ_{i,j}$, \EQUIJIT]
\label{def:equijit}
$n$ clients and $m$ days. Client $j$'s job on day $i$ has a processing time
$p_{i,j}\ge1$ and a due date $d_{i,j}\ge p_{i,j}$, and occupies exactly the interval
$(d_{i,j}-p_{i,j},\,d_{i,j}]$. Two jobs of the same day \emph{conflict} if their intervals
intersect. One machine is available on each day; a \emph{schedule} selects, for every day,
a set of clients whose jobs that day are pairwise non-conflicting (the rest are rejected
that day). Writing $Z_{i,j}$ for the indicator that client $j$ is served on day $i$, a
schedule is \emph{$k$-fair} if $\sum_iZ_{i,j}\ge k$ for every client $j$. Given $k$, is
there a $k$-fair schedule?
\end{definition}
% lax end

% lax begin lax-808846
Running times below are stated on the word RAM: a program is a fixed list of instructions
operating on words of $w$ bits, and its running time is the number of instructions it
executes on an input word, at every word length admitting that input.
% lax end

\cite{HHIMS} gives a complete answer in the fairness parameter alone:

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem1.uniform_npHard
% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem1.uniform_extremes_mem_P
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 1]
\label{thm:1}
\EQUIJIT\ is solvable in polynomial time when $k\in\{0,m-1,m\}$, and NP-hard for every
other value of $k$.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
% lax end
and then studies two restrictions — \emph{day-independent processing times}
($p_{i,j}=p_j$) and \emph{day-independent due dates} ($d_{i,j}=d_j$) — and two structural
parameters of the \emph{overall conflict graph} $G$: the graph on the clients in which two
are adjacent exactly when some day's jobs of theirs conflict.

\section{Preliminaries}

% lax begin Lax117284.ConflictGraph
A day's conflict graph is an interval graph, and a schedule is feasible on a day exactly
when its set of clients is independent in that day's graph. The \emph{overall conflict
graph} $G$ is the union, over all days, of the daily conflict graphs; its \emph{treewidth}
$\tau$ is the structural parameter of \Cref{sec:fpt}.
% lax end
% lax begin lax-689794
A tree decomposition of a graph is a tree of bags of vertices, every vertex and every edge
in some bag, the bags containing a fixed vertex forming a connected subtree; its width is
the largest bag size minus one, and the treewidth of a graph is the least width of a tree
decomposition of it. Bodlaender's algorithm finds one of minimum width, if it exists
within a given bound, in time $2^{\bigO(\tau^3)}\cdot\mathrm{poly}$.
% lax end
An algorithm is \emph{fixed-parameter tractable} (FPT) for a parameter $\kappa$ if it runs
in $f(\kappa)\cdot n^{\bigO(1)}$ time for some computable $f$.

\section{The Fairness Parameter}
\label{sec:k}

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem7.correct
% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem7.uniform_three_one_npHard
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 7]
\label{thm:7}
\EQUIJIT\ is NP-hard already at $k=1$, $m=3$, and with every job's processing time equal.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
% lax end
The reduction is from \SAT: given a formula with $\ell$ two-literal and $q$ three-literal
clauses over $n$ variables, every processing time is $2$, so two jobs of a day conflict
exactly when their due dates differ by at most one, and the whole construction places due
dates on a line, three times over — three \emph{dummy} clients, two clients $x_v^1,x_v^0$
per variable, and one client per literal occurrence, with due dates chosen so that a
$1$-fair schedule of the three days exists exactly when the formula is satisfiable.

% lax begin Lax117284.Corollary8.manyOne_freeDay
% lax begin Lax117284.Corollary8.manyOne_blockingDay
\begin{corollary}[Corollary 8]
\label{cor:8}
\EQUIJIT\ is NP-hard for every $m\ge3$ and $0<k<m-1$.
\end{corollary}
% lax end
% lax end
Two reductions lift \Cref{thm:7} to every such pair: adding one conflict-free day raises
both $m$ and the attainable fairness by one, taking $(m,k)$ to $(m+1,k+1)$; and adding one
day together with one new client that conflicts with every job of every old day, and whose
own job occupies the new day alone, forces the new client onto the new day in any $1$-fair
schedule, taking $(m,1)$ to $(m+1,1)$. Chaining these from $(3,1)$ reaches every
$(m,k)$ with $m\ge3$ and $0<k<m-1$.

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem9.manyOne_twoSat
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 9]
\label{thm:9}
\EQUIJIT\ is solved in $\bigO(mn^2+nm^2)$ time when $k=m-1$.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
A variable $x_{i,j}$ per day and client; a \emph{conflict clause}
$(\lnot x_{i,j_1}\lor\lnot x_{i,j_2})$ for every conflicting pair on a day, and a
\emph{validation clause} $(x_{i_1,j}\lor x_{i_2,j})$ for every client and every two days —
forbidding two rejections — give a 2-CNF formula satisfiable exactly when a $(m-1)$-fair
schedule exists, decided in linear time in its size by the algorithm of Aspvall, Plass and
Tarjan.

\Cref{thm:1} follows from \Cref{cor:8} and \Cref{thm:9}, together with the trivial cases
$k=0$ and $k=m$.

\section{Day-Independent Processing Times and Due Dates}
\label{sec:dayindep}

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem2.uniform_dayIndepP_npHard
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 2, hardness]
\label{thm:2hard}
\DDEQUIJIT\ is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
\Cref{thm:7}'s reduction already produces instances of equal — hence day-independent —
processing times, so \Cref{cor:8}'s lift carries this hardness to every $m\ge3,\,0<k<m-1$.

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem10.conflict_iff_d_eq_of_unitP
% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem10.hasKFairSchedule_iff_hasFullMatching
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 10]
\label{thm:10}
With unit processing times, two jobs of a day conflict exactly when their due dates agree.
An instance with $k\le m$ admits a $k$-fair schedule if and only if the bipartite graph
with a \emph{job} vertex $v_{i,j}$ per day and client, a \emph{due-date} vertex $u_{i,t}$
per day and distinct due date, and $m-k$ \emph{rejection} vertices $w_{1,j},\dots,w_{m-k,j}$
per client — joining $v_{i,j}$ to $u_{i,d_{i,j}}$ and to every rejection vertex of
client $j$ — has a matching saturating every job vertex.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
% lax end

\label{fig:bipartite}
\end{figure}

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem2.uniform_unitP_mem_P
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 2, tractability]
\label{thm:2trac}
\UEQUIJIT\ is solved in polynomial time, by \Cref{thm:10} and a maximum bipartite matching
algorithm.
\end{theorem}
% lax end

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem11.allJIT_manyOne_dayIndepD
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 11]
\label{thm:11}
\OPEQUIJIT\ is NP-hard.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
Reading an instance of $R\mid\mid\sum_jZ_j$ — unrelated machines, maximizing the
just-in-time jobs — as one of \OPEQUIJIT\ with jobs as clients and machines as days gives an
instance at $k=1$ that is a yes-instance exactly when every job of the source can be
scheduled just in time, which is NP-hard~\cite{Sung05}.

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem12.hasKFairSchedule_iff_program
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 12]
\label{thm:12}
With day-independent due dates and a constant number $m$ of days, \EQUIJIT\ is solved in
polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
Ordering the clients by due date, a dynamic program carries, for each day, the time at
which its machine next frees up; serving a client on a set of days needs each of those
machines free early enough, and leaves each free from the client's due date onwards. Since
later clients have due dates at least as large, this state is exactly what the decision
about earlier clients needs to leave behind, and for constant $m$ there are polynomially
many states.

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem13.dayGraph_eq_of_dayIndep
% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem13.chromaticNumber_dayGraph
% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem13.hasKFairSchedule_iff_mul_chromaticNumber_le
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 13]
\label{thm:13}
With both due dates and processing times day-independent, every day has the same conflict
graph $G$, and \SDEQUIJIT\ is solved in $\bigO(n\log n)$ time: a $k$-fair schedule exists
exactly when $k\cdot\chi(G)\le m$, where $\chi(G)$ is the chromatic number of $G$.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
% lax end
% lax end
A $\chi(G)$-colouring partitions the clients into $\chi(G)$ independent sets, scheduled in
rotation; conversely a clique of $G$ needs one day per client per repetition, and since $G$
is an interval graph its chromatic number equals its clique number, so the two bounds
coincide.

\section{Complexity of Structural Parameters}
\label{sec:fpt}

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem4.uniform_treewidth_npHard
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4, first bullet]
\label{thm:4a}
\EQUIJIT\ is NP-hard already at treewidth $\tau=6$ of the overall conflict graph, hence
for every $\tau=O(1)$.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
The reduction is in two steps: a per-client generalization at treewidth $4$
(\Cref{lem:14}), then a uniform-parameter reduction raising the treewidth by at most $2$
(\Cref{lem:15}).

\subsection{Hardness at bounded treewidth}

% lax begin Lax117284.MulticolouredIndepSet
\MCIS: given an $\ell$-partite graph $G=(V_1\cup\dots\cup V_\ell,E)$, is there a choice of
one vertex per class with no two chosen vertices adjacent? It is NP-hard.
% lax end
% lax begin Lax117284.MulticolouredIndepSet.normalMulticolouredIndepSet_npHard
\begin{quote}
\textbf{A gap, and its repair.} Lemma 14's reduction needs $G$ in \emph{normal form}:
$r$-regular with $r\ge1$, every class of $n\ge4$ vertices, and $|E|$ even. \cite{HHIMS}
asserts this is without loss of generality but gives no argument, and none is immediate —
the standard padding that regularizes a graph can change which multicoloured independent
sets exist. This note proves \MCIS\ NP-hard on normal-form instances directly, by a
reduction from \SAT~\cite{Tovey84}: the occurrence graph of a formula (one vertex per
clause position, grouped by variable) is made regular by five uniform \emph{ports} per
position, each a seven-vertex gadget whose independence number is fixed whether or not its
port is used, and the resulting graph is then replaced by a disjoint union of copies of
itself, one colour class per original class, which fixes the parity of $|E|$ without
touching $r$-regularity or the existence of a multicoloured independent set.
\end{quote}
% lax end

% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma14.correct
% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma14.treewidth_le
% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma14.perClient_npHard
\begin{lemma}[Lemma 14]
\label{lem:14}
$1\mid k_j,\mathrm{rep}\mid\min_j\sum_iZ_{i,j}$ — \EQUIJIT\ with one fairness parameter
$k_j$ per client, \WEQUIJIT\ — is NP-hard even when the overall conflict graph has
treewidth at most $4$.
\end{lemma}
% lax end
% lax end
% lax end
From a normal-form \MCIS\ instance $G$: $\ell(n+1)+|E|$ days — for every colour $n$
\emph{vertex days} and one \emph{validation day}, and one \emph{edge day} per edge — and
clients: a \emph{vertex client} $c_v$ ($k_{c_v}=1$) per vertex, a \emph{selection client}
$c_i$ ($k_{c_i}=1$) per colour, two \emph{edge clients} $c_{v,u},c_{u,v}$ ($k=1$) per
edge, two \emph{interaction clients} $c^+,c^-$ ($k^+=k^-=|E|/2$), and a \emph{dummy client}
$c_0$ with $k_0$ equal to the number of days. On every day, only a handful of clients take
part in that day's gadget, and $c_0$'s job — the longest of the day — \emph{blocks} every
other client not named in the gadget, forcing it to be rejected that day.

% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma14
\begin{quote}
\textbf{A second repair, to the blocking itself.} As printed, every blocked client's job on
a day is identified with every other blocked client's job on that day — literally the same
interval. Since conflict is symmetric and two clients sharing an interval conflict, this
makes \emph{every pair} of blocked clients on a day mutually conflicting, not only each
with $c_0$: the overall conflict graph the construction produces contains a clique on all
clients not named in some day's gadget, of size linear in $\ell n+|E|$, far above treewidth
$4$. The repair gives every client its own \emph{private} unit-length slot nested inside
$c_0$'s interval rather than sharing one: numbering the clients other than $c_0$ by a
bijection $\pi$ with $\{1,\dots,N\}$, $c_0$'s job has processing time $N$ and due date $r+N$
(say, on a vertex day), and a blocked client $c$'s job is the single time unit at offset
$\pi(c)$, due date $r+\pi(c)$. Every blocked client still conflicts with $c_0$ — its slot
lies inside $c_0$'s interval — but no two blocked clients conflict with each other, since
their slots are disjoint. \Cref{fig:blocking} shows the repaired gadget on one day; the tree
decomposition claimed for the construction is a decomposition of what the repaired
construction in fact builds.
\end{quote}
% lax end

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\input{figures/blocking}
\caption{The repaired blocking on one day: $c_0$'s job occupies $[0,N]$, and every other
client's job is its own private unit slot at offset $\pi(c)$ — conflicting with $c_0$ but
with no other blocked client.}
\label{fig:blocking}
\end{figure}

For every colour, the vertex and validation days form a \emph{vertex-selection} gadget
compelling exactly one vertex client per colour onto its validation day; the edge days form
an \emph{incidence-checking} gadget, in which $c^+$ and $c^-$ can together be satisfied
exactly when the selected vertices are pairwise non-adjacent. The resulting conflict graph
has the tree decomposition of \Cref{fig:treedecomp}: a root bag $\{c_0,c^+,c^-\}$, one child
bag per colour adding $c_i$, one grandchild per vertex of that colour adding $c_v$, and one
great-grandchild per neighbour $u$ of $v$ adding $c_{v,u}$ — width four throughout.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\input{figures/treedecomp}
\caption{The shape of Lemma 14's tree decomposition, for two colours.}
\label{fig:treedecomp}
\end{figure}

% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma15.correct
% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma15.treewidth_le
% lax begin Lax117284.Lemma15.perClient_manyOne_uniform
\begin{lemma}[Lemma 15]
\label{lem:15}
$1\mid k_j,\mathrm{rep}\mid\min_j\sum_iZ_{i,j}$ reduces in polynomial time to
$1\mid\mathrm{rep}\mid\min_j\sum_iZ_{i,j}$, raising the treewidth of the overall conflict
graph by at most $2$.
\end{lemma}
% lax end
% lax end
% lax end
Given $I$ with $n$ clients, $m$ days and parameters $k_j\le m$: append another $m$ days and
two clients $c^-,c^+$ whose identical job, on every new day, occupies a stretch later than
every due date of $I$ — so it conflicts with nothing of $I$ — and ask for the uniform
parameter $m$. Since $c^-,c^+$ conflict with each other and together need $m$ of the $2m$
days, each runs on exactly one day of every pair; each original client $j$ is given a job of
its own inside that stretch on $k_j$ of the new days and after it on the rest, so it is
served $k_j+(m-k_j)=m$ times overall exactly when it was served $k_j$ times in $I$. Two
clients add at most two to the treewidth.

Combining \Cref{lem:14,lem:15} gives \Cref{thm:4a}: $4+2=6$.

\subsection{Fixed-parameter tractability}

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem4.fpt_byDaysAndTreewidth
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4, second bullet]
\label{thm:4b}
\EQUIJIT\ is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to $m+\tau$, in $2^{\bigO(\tau
m)}\cdot n$ time.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
A dynamic program over a nice tree decomposition of the overall conflict graph (found by
Bodlaender's algorithm): the table entry at a bag $X$ and an assignment $\sigma$ of partial
fairness counts to $X$ records whether the clients of $X$ can be scheduled, consistently
with $\sigma$, using only the days visible below that bag.

% lax begin Lax117284.Theorem4.byClients_fptReduces_ilp
\begin{theorem}[Theorem 4, third bullet]
\label{thm:4c}
\EQUIJIT\ is fixed-parameter tractable with respect to the number $n$ of clients.
\end{theorem}
% lax end
% lax begin Lax117284.IlpClients
When $m$ is large enough that some daily conflict relation on the $n$ clients must recur,
the problem reduces to feasibility of an integer program with $2^{\bigO(n\log n)}$
variables — one per pair of a conflict \emph{type} and a subset of clients scheduled that
type, plus a slack per client — and as many constraints. \cite{HHIMS} invokes Lenstra's
algorithm for this: fixed-dimension integer programming in $n^{\bigO(n)}\cdot
m^{\bigO(1)}$ time~\cite{Lenstra83}. The formalization does not reprove Lenstra's theorem;
instead it observes that the programs this reduction produces are not arbitrary
fixed-dimension instances but one explicit family, one constraint matrix per number of
clients, and gives a direct fixed-parameter algorithm for exactly that family — guess an
assignment of the $2^{\bigO(n\log n)}$ variables within the bound the right-hand sides
impose and verify the constraints — avoiding both Lenstra's general algorithm and the
geometry of numbers it is built on.
% lax end

\section{Formalization Notes}

Running times are word RAM programs compiled, verified, from a first-order functional
language by the archive's compiler (\texttt{lax-808846}). Three dependencies are used as
black boxes and cited as concepts of other submissions, named in their own
statements: 2-satisfiability in linear time (proved independently inside this submission,
not cited), bipartite matching by Kuhn's algorithm (likewise proved independently, in the
compressed sparse row encoding of \texttt{lax-271696}), Bodlaender's and Kloks' theorem on
tree decompositions (\texttt{lax-689794}), the NP-hardness of \SAT\
(\texttt{lax-345332}, Tovey's theorem), and the NP-hardness of $R\mid\mid\sum_jZ_j$
together with \MCC\ (\texttt{lax-470956}). Besides Lean's three standard axioms, the only
further axioms used anywhere in the 84 tagged proofs are the Cook–Levin theorem and the
correctness of its encoding (\texttt{lax-429075}), the equivalence of word RAM and
Turing-machine polynomial time (\texttt{lax-759944}), and one statement of
\texttt{lax-470956} cited above — no result of the literature is taken on faith beyond
what those submissions themselves prove.

\section{Discussion}

Every theorem, lemma and corollary of \cite{HHIMS} is proved, with two repairs to the
treewidth construction of \Cref{sec:fpt} — one to the blocking mechanism, which as printed
defeats its own treewidth claim, and one supplying a proof, absent from the paper, that
\MCIS\ remains NP-hard on the regular instances the construction needs — and in both cases
what is proved is exactly the paper's stated theorem, the gadget repaired rather than the
claim weakened.
